Thirteen-year-old Amelia Wainwright has just been suspended for setting off the school fire alarm. By the end of the week, it will be the least alarming thing she's done.

A year ago, Amelia's dad vanished on a business trip, and she hasn't been herself since. She's stopped laughing, stopped trying and started getting very good at trouble. Home alone with only Wilson the Labrador for company, she finally creeps into Dad's study and plays his favourite tune on the piano.

A bookshelf swings open. Behind it is a secret room full of computers, and a letter with her name on it.

Dad was never a rare-books dealer. He's an agent for a top-secret intelligence agency, he's been captured, and the room only opens if he's still alive.

Then a mouse turns into a woman. Wilson bites the watch clean off her wrist, the watch clamps itself onto Amelia's, and before she can say 'Hang on a minute', Amelia is a cat. Which is a problem, because cats can't work door handles.

Powered by the blood of a mythical creature, the watch can turn her into any animal at all. But break its rules, and she might never be human again.

Soon Amelia and her best friend Seb (photographic memory, permanently hungry, braver than he knows) are hopping around the world by magnifying glass – long story – from Victoria Falls, where a hippo takes an instant dislike to them, to the pyramids of Egypt, with two bickering henchmen failing to keep up. To rescue her parents, Amelia will need every gadget, every animal and all the courage she can find. She'll also need to find the girl she used to be: funny, fearless and full of ideas. Some of them are even good ones.

An action-packed middle-grade spy adventure about friendship, courage and finding yourself again – perfect for readers aged 9–12 and fans of Alex Rider, Spy School, Animorphs and Percy Jackson.
